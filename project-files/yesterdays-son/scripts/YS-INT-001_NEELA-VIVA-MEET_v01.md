## Dramatic purpose

Give the audience a quiet breath while showing that connection can begin without explanation. Nee-La and ViVa arrive carrying different musical languages. Neither takes control. By listening, they discover a third language that belongs only to the encounter.

This should not feel like a concert number. It is a meeting that happens through music.

Their connection remains intentionally undefined. Deltan physiology may create or intensify attraction across species, but the scene never tells the audience whether what passes between them is pheromonal, empathic, musical, romantic, or some inseparable combination. Their attentive choices—not involuntary attraction—give the encounter its meaning.

Recovered original nuance: ViVa is specifically **not experiencing the encounter as ordinary sexual attraction**. She is startled by an intellectual and artistic pull toward a spectacular stranger. Nee-La's first saxophone response carries the friendly curiosity: **“Oh, hi. I'm an Andorian—who are you?”** The duet grows from that question.

Both characters use music for solace. Their cultures share intellectual intensity and the burden of violent histories they continue to wrestle with. The duet is therefore not merely flirtation; it is two people recognizing, without explanation, that the other understands why beauty may be necessary for survival.

## Setting

An almost-empty Enterprise lounge after hours. Most illumination comes from the windows, instrument lights, and a single warm pool around the piano. ViVa is already at the keys. Nee-La enters with her saxophone, intending only to pass through.

The location is provisional until the earlier scene is recovered.

## Beat sheet

### 1. ViVa alone — 0:00–0:15

ViVa plays a small, incomplete piano phrase. It feels private, as though she is testing whether the room will answer her. She stops before resolving it.

Her unspoken feeling: *Who is this spectacular person who has just entered—and why am I intellectually drawn toward her?*

### 2. The answer — 0:15–0:28

From outside the warm light, Nee-La quietly plays the missing final note on saxophone.

ViVa looks up. Nee-La gives a tiny, apologetic gesture: *I did not mean to intrude.*

ViVa repeats the phrase—but changes its ending, leaving a new question.

### 3. Musical conversation — 0:28–0:58

Nee-La answers. At first the instruments alternate rather than overlap:

- Piano: measured, luminous, spacious.
- Saxophone: breath, ache, blue fire.
- Piano offers structure.
- Saxophone bends the structure without breaking it.
- ViVa begins leaving deliberate openings.
- Nee-La begins listening as carefully as she plays.

The first sax phrase should feel less seductive than candid and socially open: *Hello. I'm here. Who are you?*

### 4. Recognition — 0:58–1:16

For the first time, they play together. The original phrase returns, now harmonized. A distant fragment of the opera’s three-note signal appears naturally inside their duet—as though both cultures already knew part of it.

This is the emotional turn: not romance declared, but recognition.

### 5. Interrupted resolution — 1:16–1:30

The ship’s communications system emits the same three-note signal. Both musicians stop.

ViVa softly plays the first note.

Nee-La supplies the second.

Before either can play the third, the scene cuts back into the opera.

## Optional minimal dialogue

The scene is strongest without explanatory dialogue. If a spoken exchange is needed:

**NEE-LA**
I thought you were finished.

**VIVA**
So did I.

ViVa returns her hands to the keys.

## Musical direction

- Evaluate the existing **“Quiet Orbit”** recording as the first musical source before generating a replacement.
- Begin with unaccompanied piano; introduce unaccompanied saxophone from offscreen.
- Avoid a rhythm section until the final shared passage, if one appears at all.
- Let breaths, key noise, room tone, and silence remain audible.
- The duet should grow by listening, not by becoming louder.
- Seed the opera’s three-note signal without announcing it as a theme.
- Do not end with a polished cadence. The interrupted third note hands momentum back to the larger story.

## Why it belongs

The larger opera asks whether identity survives fracture, time, and alternate worlds. This interlude gives a small human-scale answer: identity does not disappear when two people meet. It becomes harmony.

It also establishes a possible route toward the larger trio concept preserved in the earlier chats: two characters meet first through music, and Spock later joins or intersects with them. That future connection remains open rather than being forced into this first encounter.
