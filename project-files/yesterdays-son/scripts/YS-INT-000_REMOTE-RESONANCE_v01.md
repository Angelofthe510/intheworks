# Remote Resonance

Status: Preferred development concept
Version: v01
Target length: 60–90 seconds
Characters: ViVa, Deltan pianist; Nee-La, Andorian saxophonist
Form: wordless, parallel musical scene
Source music to audition first: **Quiet Orbit**

## Purpose

Introduce ViVa and Nee-La before they physically meet. They are alone in separate quarters after their shifts, unaware of one another, yet their music forms a coherent conversation across the ship.

The scene should never confirm whether the connection is Deltan empathy, Andorian sensitivity, a subspace phenomenon, the opera's time signal, or simple musical coincidence. Both women experience it as real.

## Visual grammar

Begin with separate full-frame scenes. Introduce split screen only after the first musical answer. As the connection deepens, the dividing line becomes less visually assertive, but the rooms never merge and neither performer sees the other.

## Beat sheet

### 1. End of shift — 0:00–0:12

ViVa enters her quarters, releases the posture she carries in public, and sits at her piano. Elsewhere, Nee-La is unwinding in her own quarters. Her saxophone remains in its case.

No dialogue. Only ship ambience and the small domestic sounds of two private rooms.

### 2. ViVa's question — 0:12–0:26

ViVa plays a brief, searching piano phrase. It is not a performance; it is how she clears the emotional residue of the day.

She pauses.

### 3. Nee-La hears something — 0:26–0:38

Nee-La looks up—not toward a speaker or door, but toward empty space. She cannot have literally heard the piano. After a moment, she opens the saxophone case.

Split screen begins.

Nee-La plays an answer.

### 4. The impossible duet — 0:38–1:05

ViVa reacts to the answer without understanding it. She plays again. Nee-La responds.

The two rooms remain sonically distinct at first: piano with ViVa's room tone, saxophone with Nee-La's breath and key noise. Gradually the edit lets their phrases overlap until the audience hears one duet.

Neither woman performs *at* the other. They are discovering the other phrase by phrase.

### 5. Recognition without identification — 1:05–1:18

They arrive at the same unresolved figure. Both stop at precisely the same instant.

Silence.

ViVa slowly looks around her quarters.

Nee-La lowers her sax and does the same.

Their expressions carry the same thought: *Who was just here with me?*

### 6. Trace — 1:18–1:25

A barely audible third tone remains after both instruments stop. It may resemble a Vulcan lyre harmonic or the opera's three-note signal.

Cut before either woman locates it.

## Performance notes

- ViVa begins inward and reflective, not knowingly seductive.
- Nee-La's first answer is curious and friendly: *Hello. Who are you?*
- Preserve hesitation. Their pauses are part of the conversation.
- Deltan attraction remains a possible influence, never a complete explanation.
- The final reaction may have a small edge of humor—“Who the hell was in my room?”—without breaking the mystery.
- Avoid telepathic visual clichés. No glowing heads, energy beams, or literal thought waves.

## Relationship to the trio

This is the first side of a musical triangle:

1. ViVa and Nee-La connect remotely without knowing one another.
2. They later meet physically and recognize something familiar in the other's playing.
3. Spock's Vulcan lyre introduces the third musical language.
4. The trio eventually realizes their separate motifs are parts of the same signal.

The trio should be the fuller opera sequence. **Remote Resonance** is its quiet emotional seed.

## Open decisions

- Are they aboard the Enterprise, a station, or separate nearby vessels?
- Is the third lingering tone Spock, the Guardian, or an unexplained harmonic?
- Does either character record the incident or keep it private?
- Exact placement before the physical meeting and trio formation.
